Quantum Computing

How Qubits Work

Unlike classical bits restricted to 0 or 1, qubits exploit quantum superposition to exist in multiple states simultaneously, and quantum entanglement to correlate distant qubits. This enables quantum computers to evaluate vast solution spaces in parallel for specific problem classes — not all problems, but critical ones in chemistry, cryptography, and optimisation.

IBM Condor — 1,121 Qubits

IBM's Condor processor, unveiled in late 2023 and now in active cloud deployment, is the world's largest gate-based quantum chip. Combined with IBM's Heron processor (tunable couplers for lower error rates), it forms the backbone of IBM Quantum Network's 2025 cloud offering. IBM targets fault-tolerant quantum advantage in practical applications by 2029.

AI Accelerators

NVIDIA Blackwell B200

The Blackwell B200 SXM is NVIDIA's most powerful accelerator to date — 208 billion transistors on a dual-die chip connected by NVLink-C2C. It delivers 20 petaFLOPS of FP4 performance for AI inference and 9 petaFLOPS for FP8 training. The NVL72 rack connects 72 B200 GPUs as a single logical unit — effectively a 1.4 exaFLOP AI compute unit in a single rack.

Google TPUv5e

Google's TPU v5e, available on Google Cloud since 2024, targets cost-efficient AI training and inference. With 393 teraFLOPS of BF16 performance and 1,600 GB/s high-bandwidth memory, it achieves 2× better performance-per-dollar than v4 for transformer model training at scale.

FPGAs for Adaptive AI

Intel's Agilex 9 and AMD's Versal AI Edge FPGAs enable reconfigurable AI inference for edge applications — routers, factory automation, and medical imaging — where model updates must be deployed without replacing hardware.

Storage

High-Speed Storage

NVMe Gen 5 — 14 GB/s Sequential Read

PCIe Gen 5 NVMe drives like the Crucial T705 and Samsung 990 Pro successor deliver sequential read speeds up to 14.5 GB/s — nearly double Gen 4 drives. This eliminates storage as a bottleneck for video editing, 3D rendering, and AI dataset loading tasks.

3D NAND — 162 Layers and Climbing

Western Digital's 162-layer NAND and Micron's 176-layer 3D TLC NAND push storage density to 4TB in a single M.2 2280 form factor. Analysts project 500+ layer NAND by 2027, targeting 100TB consumer drives by the early 2030s.

Storage Class Memory (SCM)

Intel Optane is discontinued, but Samsung CXL-attached DRAM and NAND-based SCM are emerging as persistent memory tiers — filling the gap between volatile DRAM (fast, expensive) and flash NVMe (slower, cheap).

3D Chip Stacking & Chiplets

Why Chiplets Matter

As monolithic die scaling becomes physically and economically unviable, chiplets allow designers to mix best-in-class components from multiple fabrication nodes and foundries into a single package. AMD's Ryzen 9000 series stacks Zen 5 CPU compute dies on a separate I/O die, while Intel's Meteor Lake uses three different TSMC and Intel process nodes in one package.

TSMC SoIC and HBM Stacking

TSMC's System-on-Integrated-Chips (SoIC) technology bonds dies face-to-face with sub-10µm pitch — enabling extremely short, high-bandwidth connections between CPU/GPU logic and SRAM or HBM memory dies. This enables NVIDIA's HBM3e stacks on Blackwell and AMD's Infinity Cache architecture.

GPU

Advanced GPUs

Real-Time Ray Tracing — Now Standard

NVIDIA's Ada Lovelace and AMD's RDNA 4 architectures both include dedicated RT cores. Ray tracing — once exclusively an offline rendering technique — now runs in real time on consumer hardware at 4K 60fps with AI upscaling, delivering cinematic light physics in gaming and design tools.

DLSS 4 & FSR 4 — AI-Driven Rendering

NVIDIA DLSS 4 uses a Transformer-based neural network (replacing CNNs) trained on the Blackwell GPU for superior temporal stability. AMD's FSR 4, also transformer-based, delivers comparable quality at open-source licensing, usable on any GPU. Both render at 25–50% of native resolution then reconstruct full quality, extending the lifespan of existing hardware.

Edge

5G & Edge Computing

Edge AI — Intelligence at the Source

Qualcomm Snapdragon X Elite, Apple M4, and NVIDIA Jetson Orin bring server-class AI inference to edge devices. The Snapdragon X Elite achieves 45 TOPS (Tera Operations Per Second) on-device — enough to run 7B parameter language models locally without cloud connectivity.

5G NR mmWave — Connecting Everything

5G mmWave deployments in urban centres deliver 4–10 Gbps peak speeds with sub-1ms latency — enabling real-time autonomous vehicle coordination, industrial robot control, and cloud-rendered XR experiences that were impossible with 4G LTE infrastructure.
